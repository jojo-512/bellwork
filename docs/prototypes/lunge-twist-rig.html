How the rig handles the hard parts

  • Legs do different jobs. Each foot is a target on the floor. The front foot never moves; the back foot travels and lands on its toes. The knees are solved from hip and foot, so feet can't slide or float.
  • Twist from the ribcage, hips square. The spine has its own twist, separate from the pelvis. The pelvis bar stays square while the shoulders and bell rotate over the front knee.
  • Gravity on the bell. The hands hold the horns at the chest and the bell always hangs straight down from them, whatever the torso does.
  • The other side is generated. Only the right-leg-back rep is authored. The left rep is a mirror, computed.
  • Rooted at the pelvis, not the ankle. That is what makes split stances work, and it is the same root a floor movement would need.

What one rep stores


      
Pelvis position, torso lean and twist, the moving foot's target and whether it's flat or on its toes. The skeleton, the knee and elbow solving, the goblet hold and the mirror are shared code, written once.
